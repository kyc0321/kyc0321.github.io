Research Directions

1. Population-based epidemiology of oral health and systemic disease

Using nationally representative NHANES survey data to investigate the interaction and shared comorbidity mechanisms between periodontitis and age-related conditions such as osteoporosis and Alzheimer's disease, with a focus on cross-system health associations in older populations — consistent with a specialist background in geriatric oral medicine and long-term care medicine.

2. Competing-risk survival methodology in cancer registry research

Developing and applying competing-risk survival models (Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard, landmark analysis) to the SEER cancer registry, comparing early-onset and late-onset prognosis across oral, head and neck, and other malignancies, with an emphasis on reproducible analytic frameworks.

3. Mendelian randomization and genetic epidemiology

Applying two-sample Mendelian randomization to test causal-inference evidence linking exposures such as gut microbiota and periodontitis to neurodegenerative conditions including Alzheimer's disease, with analysis code and data publicly archived (GitHub/Zenodo) in line with open-science practice.

4. Dental health policy and health services administration

Drawing on a health administration background and clinical dental administration experience to compare dental care reimbursement policy between Taiwan and OECD countries, published in BMJ Open with an accompanying open dataset on OSF; ongoing interest in medication safety in geriatric dental practice and quality management in institutional care.

This research integrates epidemiological methodology — competing-risk survival analysis, Mendelian randomization, and secondary analysis of large population databases — with clinical dental expertise in geriatric oral medicine, family dentistry, and special needs dentistry, in support of evidence-based, integrated oral and systemic health policy for older adults.
